# Rules (condensed from The Vote Loop Playbook by Arc Lab, CC BY-NC 4.0, arclabstudios.com)

## The loop
Hook -> Story (a chase, not a lecture) -> Cliffhanger -> Vote (2-3 physical options) -> Credit ("MOST OF YOU SAID...") opens the next part.
One rule: a vote only works if it visibly changes the next part.

## Hook (first 4-6 s)
- Frame one is mid-action, with sound. No title card, no "previously on".
- First line 7 words or fewer, second person, present tense, one stake word in red.
- Star looks into the lens in frame one.
- Credit the last vote by second 4 with a stamp.
- One wide shot by second 6: goal, threat, odds.

## Episode
- New picture every 1.5-2.5 s, nothing held longer than 3.5 s before the vote.
- Something happens about every 3 s; story turns about every 7 s.
- Kind of danger changes about every 20 s, each closer to the body.
- Ticking number all the way (distance, battery, clock). Plant it a minute before payoff.
- One spectacle at 60-65% of runtime: 3-4 s full frame, then 1-2 s silence.
- Mini-arc 75-170 s (a 30-40 s first episode is fine): hook, threat, rule, twist, cliffhanger.
- Answers release tension. Ask questions while the hero runs; don't explain while they stand still.
- At most 3 explaining lines per part, each 12 words or fewer, each paid off by action within 5 s.

## Narration
- Second person, present tense. About 4 words per sentence; half can be fragments.
- Every line names something on screen right now.
- Concrete nouns. One emotional abstract beat only.
- Never explain the mystery.
- One narrator, about 2.6 words/s. Write fewer words, don't speed up.
- Rhythm tools: countdown triplets, opposite pairs, one-word hits.

## Captions and safe zones (1080x1920)
- 1-4 words per chunk, bold condensed capitals, white with dark outline.
- One word per chunk coloured: yellow = new info, red = danger/twist. Numbers as digits.
- Safe frame: 120 px in from each side, nothing above y=270 or below y=1480. From halfway down stay left of x=860. Captions between y=1180 and 1440, max 640 px wide.
- One text layer at a time: caption OR card OR stamp.

## Vote
- Two options, three at most. Never passive.
- Plant the vote's object earlier in the episode.
- Physical verbs about what is in the last frame (ANSWER IT / RUN, not "trust him").
- Each option pulls a different instinct: curiosity, protection, escape, revenge.
- Say it three ways: spoken, big caption, numbered pills with one-line tags.
- Vote + end card under 7 s, something still moving underneath. Cut a sound off on the very last frame.
- Aim for a close split. Write both openings before you post.

## Clues
- Hide one clue per part inside the spectacle or a held background.
- Point at it, don't explain it. If nobody finds it in ~12 h, post a hint with the exact second.
- Make surprises fair: pay off something the viewer already saw.

## Covers
- Same layout every part: series label, huge PART number, two hook lines (one yellow word), a face.
- A face looking at something beats a scene. No spoilers.

## Look
- Darkness flatters simple drawings: glow, silhouettes, rim light. Target mean brightness about 50/255.
- Every shot moves. A still frame reads as a mistake.
- Build from 4-5 home setups (rear chase, front close-up, prop close-up, two-shot).
- Inserts are reactions: object, face, same object closer.

## Last check
- Check every frame for black, frozen or glitched frames.
- Watch the full final at full size as a stranger.
- No story changes after the picture is locked.

## Posting
- Caption asks the story question in plain words. The number to comment appears only in the video buttons and one pinned comment.
- Voting open at least 24 h.
- YouTube first, Instagram about an hour later.
- Pin three comments: the vote, a fan comment, a newcomer note.

## Never
Bait wording, vote in the cover or first caption line, bought followers/pods/giveaways, auto-DM tools, messaging fans first, talking episodes, 4 options or a passive one, opening on the past, confirming theories, re-uploading stitched parts, using someone else's characters or music.
